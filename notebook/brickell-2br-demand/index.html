From the Notebook · Brickell Market Snapshot · Q2 2026

The Brickell
2-bedroom sweet spot.

I mapped buyer demand for Brickell 2-bedroom condos across every price point — where active buyers are actually searching and saving listings. Unlike a single sharp cliff, this market slopes down in steps — and there’s one band where pricing up costs you almost nothing.

Written by Tayreen Nelson · Residential Real Estate Advisor · Riley Smith Group at Compass
May 2026

To build the picture below, I pulled buyer-demand readings for Brickell 2-bedroom units at a wide range of price points and combined them into a single curve. Where the same price showed up in more than one reading, the numbers lined up almost exactly — which is part of why I trust the shape here. It isn’t one noisy snapshot; it’s the same story told over and over.

The market is heavily front-loaded. Under $600K, each price point draws roughly 130 to 176 saved searches — by far the deepest pool of buyers, around two times the demand at $800K and four to five times the demand at $1.2M. For a 2-bedroom in Brickell, most of the market lives below $600K.

From there, demand doesn’t fall in one drop — it descends in three distinct steps. The first is right at $600K: cross it and demand falls from about 128 to 93, roughly a quarter of your buyers gone. The second comes around $1.2M, where it slips from about 50 to 40 and keeps grinding down through the $1.3M–$1.6M range. And the third is the steepest of all — above about $2.6M, a Brickell 2-bedroom nearly runs out of buyers entirely, settling into single digits.

But the most useful finding sits in the middle. Between roughly $800K and $1.1M, demand barely moves — it hovers around 57 to 71 the entire way. In plain terms, a Brickell 2-bedroom priced at $850K and one priced at $1.1M draw almost the same number of buyers. That’s rare, and it’s valuable: within that band, you have real room to price toward the top without thinning your audience.

In Brickell, a 2-bedroom priced at $850K or $1.1M reaches almost the same buyers. Above $2.6M, they’re gone.

What this means if you’re selling

Know which step you’re standing on. If your unit lands in the $800K–$1.1M plateau, you can price with confidence toward the upper end of that band — the buyer pool holds. But if you’re flirting with the $600K or $1.2M thresholds, a small move in the wrong direction can quietly cut your audience by a quarter. And above $2.6M, a 2-bedroom is competing for a very thin slice of the market — those buyers almost always want a third bedroom, so condition, view, and pricing have to do extraordinary work.

What this means if you’re buying

The competition you face depends entirely on price. Under $600K you’re up against the densest pool of buyers in the building — expect speed and competition. Push above $1.6M and the field thins quickly, which means more negotiating room and more time to decide. The higher you go, the more leverage you have.

For sellers

The $800K–$1.1M band is your friend — demand holds flat, so price toward the top. Watch the $600K and $1.2M thresholds.

For buyers

Under $600K is a fight; above $1.6M the field thins fast and your leverage grows.

One honest caveat worth stating plainly: this data measures buyer interest — searches and saved listings — not closed sales. But interest is the leading edge of a market. It’s where pricing strategy begins, well before a single offer is written.
