Connections

We don't build it. We know everyone who does.

It starts with what you are actually trying to do — live in it, hold it as an investment, or build on it. From there, a new-construction project runs on a bigger cast than a resale, and what we bring is the roster: the architects, builders, arborists, lenders and attorneys who actually do this work in the Grove, and the vocabulary to talk to all of them. Victor speaks to the design and construction side; Tayreen works with the surveyor, the title company and your closing attorney.

Here is who is involved, and what each of them does.

Architect & designer

Draws the residence and carries the design intent through construction and finish selections.

General contractor

Builds it — holding the schedule, the trades, and the cost of every detail on the drawings.

Surveyor

Establishes boundaries, setbacks and elevation, and issues the certificates lenders and insurers rely on.

Tree survey & arborist

Identifies every tree on the lot and which are protected. Under the Grove's canopy, removing or relocating a specimen takes a permit — and what stays often shapes where the house can sit.

Municipality

Issues permits, runs inspections, and grants the certificate of occupancy.

Lender & appraiser

Finances the project and values the finished residence.

Title & closing attorney

Clears title, handles lien releases, and closes the transaction.

Insurance

Places the policy, using wind mitigation and elevation data from the survey.

Inspector

Independent eyes on the work at the stages that matter most.

Your professionals stay yours. We work alongside your own attorney, accountant and advisors — we do not replace them, we do not provide legal or tax advice, and we do not manage construction. Where you need someone you do not already have, we can tell you who is worth your time.

How We Work

Four ways clients work with us.

01

Buying to live in it

You are buying a set of drawings and a promise. We walk the site with you at each stage, translate the finish schedule into what you will actually live with, and negotiate the terms that matter in a build contract — deposit structure, completion language, allowance and change-order treatment, warranty, and what happens if the date slips.

02

Buying as an investment

Not every new build holds its value. We price it against what has actually closed nearby, flag the finishes and floor plans that date fastest, and are straight with you about what a given street will support.

03

Land to build on

We find lots — including ones not widely shopped — and put the right people on them early: a surveyor for the boundaries and protected trees, an architect for what zoning and setbacks allow, an engineer for elevation. Once that comes back, we read what it means for value — what the block supports once the house is finished — and we represent you through the purchase.

04

Selling a completed home

Finished a build, or bought one and ready to move on? A new-construction sale is not a resale listing. It is priced, photographed, and told differently — and the buyer pool is smaller, sharper, and largely reachable before the property is ever public.
